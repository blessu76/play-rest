Every game offers a careful move and a bold one. The careful move has a small, steady outcome. The bold move has a wide range, from very good to very bad. Which one is correct is not a matter of personality. It depends on where you stand.

## The rule of thumb

When you are ahead, you want fewer surprises. The careful move shrinks the range of outcomes, and a narrow range favors the leader. When you are behind, you need surprises. A steady path leads to a steady loss, and only a wide range gives you a chance to catch up.

So the sensible habit is simple: play safe when ahead, take a chance when behind.

## The hard part: knowing where you are

The rule needs an honest reading of the position, and that is where players go wrong. Some feel behind when they are even and take needless risks. Others feel ahead because the last few moves went well and relax when they should not.

A quick check helps. Imagine the game continuing with both sides playing sensibly. Who would you expect to win? If the answer is "I would", be careful. If it is "they would", look for something bold. If you honestly cannot tell, keep your options open.

## Risk in short rounds

In a short round, being behind carries a special pressure: there is little time left to recover. That makes the bold move more tempting and, usually, more justified than in a long game. The same shortness makes a failed gamble final, so the question to ask is: if this fails, am I any worse off than if I had played safe and lost anyway?

If the answer is "no", gamble. If the answer is "yes, and I was not really behind", think again.

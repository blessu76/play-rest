Every game on play.ipe.rest is played alone. You are by yourself, or you are against an AI opponent, and no one else is in the room. That was not an accident, and it is not only a limit. It is a choice with benefits and costs, and both are worth writing down.

## What alone gives a break

A break often comes without warning and leaves without notice. Playing alone means that nobody has to be there at the same time as you. There is no match to wait for, no friend who has to be free, no turn that sits unanswered because the other person went to lunch.

It also means no pressure to be good in front of someone. You can play badly. You can stop in the middle. Nobody is left waiting while you answer a message.

And it often means less to carry. Inkisle needs no account and no network, and Iltonggi's core features work offline, with only the weather being optional on the internet. When a game does not depend on anyone else's connection, it can open anywhere.

## What alone costs

The honest list is longer. A game against an AI is a different thing from a game against a person, and some players will want the person. There is no friendly rivalry, no winning against a specific friend, no shared story to retell afterwards.

We have said this on the cards for our games. Claimball and Sunmeld are always a duel against an AI, and their cards say they are not for anyone who wants to play people (the Sunmeld card adds anyone who wants to build up solo records). Inkisle is solo and offline for now. Online play, in the words of its site, will be decided later, based on how players respond. Iltonggi is a solo turn-based game as well.

So for now, if playing with friends is what you want from a game, ours are not the right ones. We would rather you hear that from us than download something and find out.

## Why a solo game can still feel social

One thing we have noticed in how we write about the games: a solo game still has a person on the other end of it. The designer who set the rules, the tester who found the problem, and the AI opponent who is meant to play fair are all there. A journal like this one is partly about that, a way of showing who made the thing and how.

We would not claim this replaces a friend. It is a smaller thing than that, and we think it is still a real one.

## Not forever, not promised

We are not promising multiplayer. We are not ruling it out either. Inkisle's site says online play is decided later. For the others we have nothing to announce, and we will not invent a plan for the sake of a tidier ending.

None of the games mentioned here is released. Iltonggi and Claimball are marked "coming soon", and Inkisle and Sunmeld are in development, with no store pages or release dates yet.

If a game you can start and stop alone, with nothing to wait for, sounds like the right size for a break, Inkisle is a good card to open on [play.ipe.rest](https://play.ipe.rest).

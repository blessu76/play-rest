In many turn-based games, the player who moves first has an edge. They get to act before anything has been answered. If the game is otherwise even, that small head start can be enough to tilt the result. People have found several ways to balance it.

## Komi: pay the second player

In Go, Black plays first and White receives a bonus in points, called komi, to compensate. The size of the bonus is a matter of the ruleset, and it has been adjusted over time. A figure with a half point, such as 6.5, is common because it removes ties. The principle matters more than the number: the first move is worth something, so give something to the other side.

## Handicap: pay the weaker player

When two players are not equally strong, Go lets the weaker side start with stones already placed on the board. As a rough guide, the number of stones tracks the difference in rank. The game is made even by changing the starting position rather than the rules.

## The pie rule: let the divider choose second

The third idea is the neatest. One player makes the first move, and then the other player may either accept the position or swap sides. Think of cutting a pie: the person who cuts does not know which piece they will get, so they cut as fairly as they can.

The pie rule needs no number and no measurement of strength. It only needs the first mover to be honest with themselves about what is fair, because they will be the one living with the result.

## What a short game can borrow

When a game is played against an AI, as in [Sunmeld](https://sunmeld.ipe.rest), fairness has a quiet form. The opponent cannot negotiate, so the balance has to be built in, in how the game sets up and who begins. We do not describe those choices here because our sites do not specify them, and we would rather not guess.

[Iltonggi](https://iltonggi.ipe.rest) shows a different kind of balance. You choose your kingdom and difficulty at the start, and in the first two rounds no enemy attacks. That is a small gift from the game to the player who is still finding their feet. It is a way of making the first move fair, even if it does not look like komi.

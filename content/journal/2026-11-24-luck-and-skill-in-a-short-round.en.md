If you flip a coin ten times, you will probably not get exactly five heads. If you flip it a thousand times, you will be much closer to half. Chance evens out slowly, and that is the whole difficulty with luck in a short game.

## Short rounds are noisy

In a long game, a bad break is one event among hundreds, and skill has time to show. In a round of a few minutes, one unlucky moment can be a large share of everything that happens. The result looks decisive, but it is based on little.

That does not mean short games are bad. It means a single result in a short game tells you less about your play than it feels like.

## Two ways to blame

When you lose, there are two easy stories. One is "it was luck". The other is "I played badly". Each can be true, and each is tempting for the wrong reasons. Blaming luck protects your pride and teaches you nothing. Blaming yourself completely can drive you to change a perfectly good habit.

A more useful question is smaller: was there a moment where a different choice would have done better? If yes, that moment is what you can learn from. If not, you were unlucky and can move on.

## Judging yourself over several rounds

Because one short round is noisy, judge across several. If a particular plan works in most of your rounds, it is probably good. If it works once and fails three times, you have learned something even if the one success felt great.

This is also why a game that lets you restart quickly is useful for learning. More rounds mean a clearer picture.

## Reducing luck is a design choice

Designers can change how much chance matters. Hiding part of the board adds uncertainty, though uncertainty is not the same as luck. In [Iltonggi](https://iltonggi.ipe.rest) there is fog and there are spies, so you decide with partial information. That is a skill: reading what you can see and guessing what you cannot. In [Sunmeld](https://sunmeld.ipe.rest), where you and an AI alternate turns under four lines of rules, every opponent has its own habits, so learning those habits is a skill too.

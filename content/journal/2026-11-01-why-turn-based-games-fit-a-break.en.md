A break is a small piece of time with an edge you did not choose. The bus arrives, the kettle clicks, someone says your name. Turn-based games fit that shape better than most, and the reason is simpler than it sounds.

## Pausing costs nothing

In a real-time game, the world keeps moving whether or not you do. Look away for a moment and something happens without you. That is part of the fun, and it is also exactly what a break does not allow, because breaks get interrupted.

In a turn-based game, the world waits for your move. Nothing happens while you glance at the departure board, answer a message, or just stare out of the window. You come back to the same board and the same choices. The game never asks you to hurry back, and it never punishes you for having a life around it.

That is the first thing a break needs from a game: stopping is free.

## Time to think is the point

Turn-based games trade reflexes for decisions. You do not need quick thumbs. You need to look at a position and choose. Where do I strike? What am I leaving open? What am I not seeing yet?

We will not claim that thinking is the same as resting, and we have no study to cite. Our view is simpler. For many people, a small problem with a clear shape is a good place to put attention for a few minutes, and a turn-based game offers one without a clock pushing on you. Taking your time is part of the design, not a cost of it.

## Short rounds, clear ends

Turn-based is not automatically short. Plenty of turn-based games run for hours and expect you to remember what you were planning last week. For a break, you want a round that ends.

[Iltonggi](https://iltonggi.ipe.rest) is our turn-based game: a short, fast, classic territory-conquest strategy game set in Korea's Three Kingdoms era. A round takes 5 to 10 minutes. Closing the app saves your game automatically, and nothing nags you to log in every day. Land you have not visited stays in the fog until you send a spy, so each turn is a choice made with what you know and no more. In a battle you can step in with direct command or leave it to auto, which means you decide how much attention a given turn deserves.

Iltonggi is not out yet. It is marked "coming soon", and the iPhone version comes first. We would rather say that plainly than let this read like a launch notice.

## Not everything we make takes turns

[Sunmeld](https://sunmeld.ipe.rest) is a duel in alternating turns: you and an AI opponent take turns dropping stones into one shared tub, matching stones merge, and if the tub overflows, the player who placed the last stone loses. [Inkisle](https://inkisle.ipe.rest) and [Claimball](https://claimball.ipe.rest) run in real time, and they lean on other things that suit a break: short rounds, simple rules, and in Inkisle's case a portrait screen you can play with one thumb.

So turn-based is not the only way to respect a break, and it is not for everyone. If you want fast, real-time play, a turn-based game will feel slow, and the Iltonggi card on [play.ipe.rest](https://play.ipe.rest) says exactly that. What we try to keep across all four games is the same idea: the game should fit inside your break instead of taking it over.

Three of our four games are about taking land. We did not plan it that way as a theme. It happened because the idea keeps proving itself: the board has parts, you make some of them yours, and you can see at a glance how far you have got.

The old land-grab game, where you draw lines in the dirt and flick a stone to carve out a little more territory, has rules that fit in a few lines. It is easy to explain and hard to put down. That combination is what we keep chasing.

## Why land works for short play

Territory is a score you can see. You do not have to read a number to know who is ahead, because the picture tells you. Progress is physical: the board changes, and it changes in your favor or not.

It also gives a round a natural shape. You start with a little, you take more, and either the board is yours or it is not. That is a beginning, a middle and an end, which a short game needs.

## Towns: Iltonggi

[Iltonggi](https://iltonggi.ipe.rest) is a short, fast, classic territory-conquest strategy game set in Korea's Three Kingdoms era. The map works town by town. You tap a town to gather and build up soldiers, then attack the neighbors. Bigger towns gather more troops, so the capital is large and the countryside is small. That one difference is enough to make you ask what to take first and what to defend.

It is turn-based, and land you have not visited is covered in fog. It also does not ask you to swallow every scrap of land: our blog says one decisive move can end a game, which suits a short round.

## Islands: Inkisle

[Inkisle](https://inkisle.ipe.rest) moves the idea onto the sea. You send fleets between islands and tint the sea your color. Islands make troops, and a larger force arriving takes the island. It runs in real time, so land changes hands while you watch.

The conquest is quicker and more physical. You drag, the fleet goes, the island changes color. Where Iltonggi asks "what shall I take?", Inkisle asks "can I get there in time?"

## Tiles: Claimball

[Claimball](https://claimball.ipe.rest) puts the same idea on a notebook grid. The tile where your ball bounces becomes your land, and if you miss, you lose land. Hitting the ball with the middle of your paddle is a smash that claims three tiles at once.

Here land is the smallest it gets: single squares.

## The fourth game

[Sunmeld](https://sunmeld.ipe.rest) is not a land game. You and an AI opponent share one tub of stones, and the question is not what to take but what to leave. We mention it because it shows that territory is a choice, not a rule of our studio.

## Honest notes

Land-grab games are not new, and we are not claiming to have invented anything. What we hope to offer is the shape at a small scale: land you can read, rounds you can finish, and rules short enough for a break. None of the four is released. Iltonggi and Claimball are marked "coming soon", and Inkisle and Sunmeld are in development.

If you would like the slowest and most map-like of the three, begin with Iltonggi on [play.ipe.rest](https://play.ipe.rest).

A lot of puzzle games add a clock because a clock is easy to design. Dusk Blocks leaves it out. You take as long as you like.

## How it is meant to play

You have an 8 by 8 board and three pieces at a time. You place them so that full rows and columns clear, and then three new pieces arrive. When none of the pieces can fit, the round ends. There are about twenty piece shapes, from single squares to long bars and L and T shapes.

## No clock

A break is already short, so a timer on top of it asks you to hurry through your own rest. Without one, a round can be five seconds of thought or a long look at the board, and neither is wrong. You can set the phone down mid-round and come back to the same board.

## Possible, every time

A puzzle you cannot win is just a way to feel bad. So the pieces are designed so that each new set of three can always be placed, in some order, on the board as it stands. A round can still end, but it ends because of your choices and not because the game dealt you something impossible.

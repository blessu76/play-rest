A blank page is harder to write on than a lined one. Games work the same way. When anything is possible, no choice feels important. A limit turns a loose activity into a question you can actually answer.

## Limits you can count

Take a fixed number of turns. If you know the game ends after a set number, every turn has a price. Spending one on something small is a real cost, because you can count what is left.

[Iltonggi](https://iltonggi.ipe.rest)'s first game is 20 turns. That number gives the round a shape. You can see the end from the start, plan toward it, and notice when you are running late.

## Limits you can see

A small board is a different kind of limit. [Claimball](https://claimball.ipe.rest) plays on a 6 by 10 notebook grid. With only sixty tiles, a single smash that claims three of them is a big event. On a huge map it would be a rounding error.

A small space also keeps everything in view. You do not scroll to find what matters. You look, and it is there.

## Limits you can read

The third kind is a limit on the rules themselves. [Sunmeld](https://sunmeld.ipe.rest) has four lines of rules, and [Inkisle](https://inkisle.ipe.rest) has one page. A small rulebook leaves less to remember and more room to think. Cutting a rule is often harder than adding one, and the result is easier to learn.

## How to use a limit as a player

Treat the limit as information. Ask what it asks you to give up. A turn cap says "do not dawdle". A small board says "your choices crowd one another". A short rulebook says "the depth is in the combinations".

Then plan backwards from the limit. If the game ends at turn 20, what must be true by turn 10? If the board is small, which space will be contested first?

## When a limit is too much

A limit can also be harsh. A cap that is too tight leaves no room to recover from a mistake, and a board that is too small repeats itself. Good limits are chosen to be generous enough to forgive and tight enough to matter. We cannot promise ours get that balance right before players have tried them.

None of the games mentioned here is released. Iltonggi and Claimball are "coming soon", and Inkisle and Sunmeld are in development.

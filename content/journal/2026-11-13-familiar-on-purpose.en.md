There is a pressure on every game to be new. New genre, new mechanic, a trailer that says nobody has seen this before. We feel it too. But for a game meant to fit into a few spare minutes, new is expensive, and it is worth asking what exactly a player pays for it.

## The price of strange

A brand-new kind of game has to be learned from the ground. Even with simple rules, the player has to build a picture of how the thing behaves. That takes time, and a spare minute is a poor place to spend it.

A familiar shape does the opposite. If you already know roughly what dropping a stone, matching a pair or taking a turn means, you can start almost at once. The first minute goes to playing, not to understanding.

## Where the new idea goes

That does not mean a game should have no idea of its own. It means the idea should go in one place and the rest should be friendly. A small twist on a known shape is easier to notice and easier to love than a pile of unfamiliar things.

## Sunmeld, openly

[Sunmeld](https://sunmeld.ipe.rest) is a good example, and it is plain about it. Dropping stones and merging matching ones into the next stage is a familiar kind of game, and we do not claim a brand-new genre. The twist is elsewhere.

The first twist is that you share one tub with an AI opponent. Every stone you place changes the space the other side has to work in, and the other way around. The second is that the duel is in alternating turns, with a clear end: if the tub overflows, the player who placed the last stone loses.

The rules take four lines. Because the base is familiar, those four lines are quick to take in, and the interesting part, the question of what you leave behind, is where your attention goes. The game's site also says every opponent has its own habits, so the same tub plays out differently each time.

## Why we say so

Our card for Sunmeld on play.ipe.rest lists who it is not for, and one entry is anyone who expects a brand-new kind of game. We put it there on purpose. It would be easy to dress up a known shape as a revolution, and that would sit badly with the way we want to talk about our games. Better that you know what you are getting.

The same goes for the rest of the status. Sunmeld is in development, and it is not released. There is no store page and no release date, and we do not state a round length because the game's site does not list one.

## A small test

If you want a rule of thumb, try this: can you describe the game to a friend in the time it takes to walk across a room? If you can, a spare minute is enough to start playing it. If the answer is a long one, the idea may be wonderful, but it is asking for more than a break has.

If that sounds like your kind of start, Sunmeld on [play.ipe.rest](https://play.ipe.rest) is where to look.

Ask how a game ends and you learn how it should be played. The ending quietly decides what every earlier move is for. Here are three common kinds.

## Ending by score

When the game stops and you count, the middle is about accumulating. Every move adds a little or denies a little, and the final difference is what matters.

This kind forgives. A mistake costs points, not the game, and a late rally can still win. It suits a relaxed break, because there is always something to play for. It can also feel anticlimactic, since the winner is sometimes clear long before the end.

[Claimball](https://claimball.ipe.rest)'s board reads a little like a running tally: tiles you claim are yours, and a missed ball loses land. We use it only as an illustration of the feeling, not as a statement of its win condition.

## Ending by elimination

When the game ends because one side has nothing left, the middle is about survival. You are not building a score, you are keeping a position alive.

This kind is sharp. A single collapse can end everything, and a lead can disappear quickly. It suits a break where you want tension, though it can feel harsh if the round was long.

The territory-grabbing shape of [Iltonggi](https://iltonggi.ipe.rest) has some of this edge: when a town changes hands, you can see what is at stake. Again, this is a feeling, not a rule we are quoting.

## Ending by the last move

The third kind is the oddest. The game ends when the board runs out of room, and whoever made the final move loses. In [Sunmeld](https://sunmeld.ipe.rest), whoever places the last stone as the tub overflows loses.

Here the middle is about timing. Neither side wants to be the one who runs out of safe moves, so every placement is a question about who will be forced first. It is short, tense and easy to explain, and it rewards the habit of looking a couple of moves ahead.

## Choosing by the ending

If you want something to relax with, look for a score. If you want to feel the edge, look for elimination. If you want a small, clean puzzle with a sting at the end, look for a last-move rule.

The idea behind all of these pieces is the same. A short game is not a smaller long game. It is a different shape, and the ending is the part of the shape that you feel most.

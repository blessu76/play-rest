Most short games forget you the moment a round ends. Patchland is built around the opposite idea: every round you win leaves something behind on a map that is yours.

## Three ways to take land

Patchland is a territory game played against AI lords, and it does not use one rule for every fight. As currently designed, a region on the map decides which of three ways you play it.

- **Trace.** You draw a line on the board and win the ground you enclose. It is quick and a little nervous, because a careless line can be cut.
- **Place.** You and the lord take turns putting pieces on a hex board, and a piece can flip what it touches. It is slower and closer to a puzzle.
- **Spread.** You and the lord grow from opposite corners, and whoever claims more than half first wins.

Three small rules instead of one big one is the point. A break can be a hurried minute or a calm five, and Patchland offers both without asking you to choose a mode.

## A map that keeps score for you

The win is only half of it. Each region you take is covered in your color on a single map, so after a week of short rounds you can see where you have been. There is no ranking in it and nobody to compare with. The map is simply a record of your own spare minutes.

## Against lords, not people

The opponents are six quirky AI lords. Patchland is played alone and works offline. If you want to play against friends, it is not the right game.

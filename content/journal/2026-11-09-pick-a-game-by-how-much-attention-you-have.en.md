We usually choose games by genre: strategy, puzzle, action. For a break, that is not the most useful question. A better one is smaller and more honest: how many minutes do I have, and how much of me is available?

Some breaks give you ten minutes and a calm head. Some give you three minutes and one hand. Some give you only a gap between two sentences. A game that is wonderful in the first can be wrong for the third.

Here is a plain way to choose, using our four games as examples. They are not released yet, so read this as a guide to how each one is meant to be used, not as a review.

## Ten minutes, and you want to think slowly

[Iltonggi](https://iltonggi.ipe.rest) is turn-based, so the world waits while you decide. A round is meant to take 5 to 10 minutes. It is the game for a seat where nothing is asking for you, and you want to look at a map and choose where to strike. Its card on play.ipe.rest also says who it is not for: if you want fast, real-time play, a turn-based game will feel slow.

## A few minutes, one hand

[Inkisle](https://inkisle.ipe.rest) is built for the standing-up moments. A round takes about 3 to 5 minutes, is played upright with one thumb, and needs no account or network. It runs in real time, so it is not a game for slow, careful turns, and the card says so.

## A few minutes, and you want a one-on-one

[Sunmeld](https://sunmeld.ipe.rest) is a duel in alternating turns against an AI opponent, sharing one tub. The rules take four lines. We do not give a round length, because the game's site does not list one. It suits a moment when you want something with a bit of head in it, but not a map.

## Very short, and your hands are happy to be busy

[Claimball](https://claimball.ipe.rest) is meant for short gaps. Its matches are meant to be short, and we do not quote a time, since the figure is a proposal before playtesting. You return a ball in real time, so your hands are in it. If busy-handed games are a burden for you, it is not the right pick.

## Why this is a better question than genre

Genre tells you what a game is made of. Minutes and attention tell you whether it will fit. We would rather you leave with the right game for ten minutes of quiet than the right genre for the wrong moment.

This guide is also a limit. None of the four is played with friends, and none is released. Each is solo or against an AI, and on play.ipe.rest Iltonggi and Claimball are marked "coming soon" while Inkisle and Sunmeld are marked "in development". If you are not sure which fits, start with whichever matches the time you have right now.

If that time is a few minutes and you would like some head-work against a patient opponent, Sunmeld is a place to begin.

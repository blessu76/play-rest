Many puzzle games are private. You have your own board, your own mess, your own score, and nobody else can touch any of it. That is relaxing, and it has a particular limit: nothing you do matters to anyone but you.

A duel on a shared board is the opposite. Whatever you leave behind is what your opponent has to work with.

## Your move is their terrain

Think of the oldest board games. When you put a piece down, you do two things at once. You make progress toward your own goal, and you change the position the other side has to answer. The best move is rarely the one that looks best for you alone.

This is the part of a duel we find interesting at small scale. You do not need a big board or a long game for it. You only need one space that both sides use, and a rule about whose turn it is.

## How Sunmeld sets it up

[Sunmeld](https://sunmeld.ipe.rest) is a merge duel against an AI opponent in one shared tub. The rules are four lines. You take turns dropping stones. When stones of the same stage touch, they merge into the next stage. Whoever merges scores. If the tub overflows, the player who placed the last stone loses.

Put those together and the shape you stack stops being only yours. A tidy pile helps you, but it is also the room your opponent has to work in. A clumsy pile can be your problem and theirs at once, and when the tub gets full, the last stone is a real decision, because the loser is the player who placed it.

On the game's site we describe the appeal as the taste of working out what you leave behind. We have not found a shorter way to put it.

## Why this suits a break

A shared board can sound like a heavy idea. In practice it makes a short game feel two-sided from the first turn. You do not wait for the opponent to do something unrelated to you somewhere else on the screen. Every turn they take lands in your space, and every turn you take lands in theirs.

Because turns alternate, you also have time to read what just happened.

## What we cannot yet say

Sunmeld is in development and not released, and there is no store page or release date. We do not quote a round length for it because the game's site does not list one. Other details are still being tested, so we are not describing them either. The shared tub is the idea the game is built on. How well that idea plays is not something a journal can settle.

If "what do I leave behind for them?" sounds like a fun question for a few minutes, Sunmeld is the game on [play.ipe.rest](https://play.ipe.rest) that asks it.

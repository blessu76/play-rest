Playing against a computer sometimes gets described as the lesser option, the thing you do when no person is around. We see it differently, at least for the kind of game we make. For a few spare minutes, an AI opponent has some advantages that a person cannot offer.

## What a computer opponent gives you

It is always there. You do not arrange a time, wait for the other side to take a turn, or find out halfway through that they have left. The match starts when you do and stops when you do.

There is no audience. You can lose badly, try a strange idea, or play half-attentively on a bus, and nobody sees it. For anyone who has felt a little tense in front of a stranger, that is a real relief.

And it can grow with you. A person at your level is hard to find on demand. An opponent built as part of the game can be arranged in steps.

## The steps in Claimball

[Claimball](https://claimball.ipe.rest) is a casual duel against an AI rival on a notebook grid. The tile where your ball bounces becomes your land, and if you miss, you lose land. Every match is you against an AI rival.

The plan is for the rivals to be grouped into leagues, so you meet harder ones as you go. We do not give the exact number of rivals or leagues here, because those figures are proposals before launch and may change. The idea matters more than the count: an opponent that does not stay the same, so that a few spare minutes always have a next step.

## What we try not to do

An AI rival is worth practicing against only if it is honest. We said this in our first post and repeat it here, because it matters most in exactly this kind of game. A comeback should come from the rules, not from an opponent that was quietly weakened to let you win. A loss should be a loss you can learn from.

We cannot yet show you how that feels in practice. Claimball is marked "coming soon". It has no store page and no release date.

## Not for everyone

A computer opponent is a different thing from playing with friends, and none of ipe()'s four games has a mode for playing people at the moment. Claimball is also played in real time, returning the ball, so it is not for anyone who finds busy-handed games a burden. The game's card on play.ipe.rest says so. If you want to play people online, this is not the game for that.

## A sparring partner

The best AI opponents in short games act like sparring partners: always ready, never impatient, and a bit better than you were yesterday. That is what we want Claimball's rivals to be.

If a quick duel with something that is always ready sounds right, the [Claimball card on play.ipe.rest](https://play.ipe.rest) is where to look.

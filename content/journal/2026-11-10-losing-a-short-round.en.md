If a game has an opponent, you will lose to it sometimes. That is not a flaw. A win that could not have been a loss would mean nothing. But how a loss feels depends a great deal on how long the round was, and on whether you understand why it happened.

## The price of a loss

In a game that takes an evening, a loss costs an evening. In a game that takes a few minutes, a loss costs a few minutes. That sounds obvious, but it shapes everything about how a defeat lands. After a short round you can shrug, learn a little, and either start another or put the phone away. Nothing large has been spent.

This is one reason we like short rounds in games with opponents. The stakes of a single round are small enough that you are free to try something odd.

## A loss you can read

The second thing that matters is whether the loss makes sense. The bad kind of defeat is the one where you cannot say what happened. The good kind is one where, looking back, you can point to the move.

Some of our rules make the ending easy to point at. In Sunmeld, when the shared tub overflows, the player who placed the last stone loses. You can see the tub, you can see the stone, and you know which turn it was. In [Claimball](https://claimball.ipe.rest), a miss costs you land, and the land is exactly what the match is about. In [Iltonggi](https://iltonggi.ipe.rest), our blog says we kept the satisfaction of a game that one decisive move can end, like the fall of a king in janggi, which suits short rounds. A loss that comes from a single visible move is a loss you can learn from.

## Losing versus being cheated

The third thing is trust. You can accept losing to an opponent that played well. It is much harder to accept losing to one that cheated, or to one that was quietly made stronger behind your back. It is just as unsatisfying to win against one that was quietly made weaker.

We hold our AI opponents to a simple rule: comebacks come from the rules, not from a secretly adjusted opponent. This is a promise about how the game decides who wins. It is not a promise that you will win.

## What we cannot claim

We cannot tell you yet that our games get this right. None of the four is released. Claimball is marked "coming soon", Iltonggi too, and Inkisle and Sunmeld are in development, with no store pages or release dates. A loss that feels fair on paper can still feel unfair in someone's hands, and we will only find out by watching people play.

What we can say is what we aim for: short enough to cost little, clear enough to read, and honest enough to trust. If that is the kind of loss you would like to have in a spare few minutes, Claimball is a good card to open on [play.ipe.rest](https://play.ipe.rest).

Everyone who has played tic-tac-toe remembers the day it stopped being interesting. Nobody told you the trick. At some point you noticed that if you both play without a mistake, the game always ends in a draw, and after that every game was just a small test of whether the other person would slip.

That feeling has a name. Tic-tac-toe is a solved game.

## What "solved" means

A game is solved when the result of perfect play is known. In tic-tac-toe, perfect play from both sides ends in a draw. That is easy to check by hand, which is why children work it out sooner or later.

Bigger games can be solved too, but it takes a great deal more effort. Checkers (the 8x8 English version, also called draughts) was solved by a research team led by Jonathan Schaeffer, who reported the result in 2007: with perfect play from both sides, checkers is a draw. It took years of computer work. Chess, by comparison, has not been solved.

Solving a game does not mean people can play it perfectly. It means that someone, or something, knows what perfect play would produce.

## Why a solved game can still be played

For a person, a game is "solved" in a looser sense long before any proof. Once you know the pattern, you stop deciding and start remembering. The game still exists, but the part that made it a game has gone out of it.

Checkers is a useful reminder. It is solved, and people still play it, because no person plays perfectly. The proof describes a game nobody actually plays.

Tic-tac-toe is different because a child can get close to perfect. A game this small has nowhere to hide.

## What a short game needs

This is the danger for any small game. A short round with few rules is easy to learn, and what is easy to learn is easy to master. The thing that keeps it alive is rarely a bigger board. It is a reason for the next game to differ from the last.

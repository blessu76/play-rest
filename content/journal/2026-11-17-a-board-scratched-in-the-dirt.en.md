The least equipment a board game has ever needed may be a few handfuls of earth. In a large family of games usually grouped under the name mancala, the board can be nothing more than two rows of holes scooped out of the ground, and the pieces can be seeds or pebbles. When the game is over, you smooth the dirt over with your hand.

We find that a useful picture to keep in mind when we think about games for spare minutes.

## What the family looks like

Mancala games are sometimes called sowing games. Players take turns picking up the pieces from one pit and dropping them one by one into the pits that follow, and the aim, depending on the version, is to capture pieces or to end up holding more of them. They are played across much of Africa and parts of Asia, and the rules differ from place to place and from one player to the next. Some historians count them among the oldest board games in the world. How old exactly is debated, so we will not give a date.

Many versions are passed on by showing, not by writing down. A game that can be explained in a few sentences and learned by watching one round does not need a manual.

## What it asks of a player

Think about what is missing. There is no account, no download, no screen, and no one waiting for you to finish. You need a flat patch of ground, a few seeds and a person to take turns with. The whole game fits in an afternoon break, and you can stop whenever you like because the board is just the ground.

It is also a game of taking turns, where each move changes what the other player can do next, which is the same idea that keeps much richer games interesting.

## What a phone game can borrow

We do not say that a phone game should be as bare as a hole in the dirt. We do think the old games show which parts of a game are essential and which are decoration. The essential parts here are short rules, a board that explains itself, and turns you can think about.

That is the standard we try to hold our own games to. [Inkisle](https://inkisle.ipe.rest) needs no account and no network, and its rules fit on one page: islands make troops, and a larger force arriving takes the island. [Sunmeld](https://sunmeld.ipe.rest) states its rules in four lines. Neither is as light as a handful of seeds, but it is the same direction.

## Honest limits

We are not historians of these games, and we are not saying our games descend from them. A pit-and-pebble game and a merge duel have little in common beyond taking turns and fitting in a break. And none of our games is out yet: Inkisle and Sunmeld are in development, with no store pages or release dates.

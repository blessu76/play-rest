When a game gets harder, the usual way is to make things bigger. More enemies, higher numbers, tighter time. It works, but it has a cost for a game people play in short bursts: if you come back after a few days, the climb can feel steep, and you have forgotten how the old level worked.

There is another way to raise difficulty. Keep the numbers modest and add one new idea at a time.

## Why one rule is enough

A rule is something a player has to learn, hold in their head and use. Each new rule asks for a small bit of attention, and a game for spare minutes does not have much attention to spare. If you add a single rule per step, a player can take it in during one round and use it in the next.

It also makes difficulty honest. When a round is harder because of a rule you can name, you know what to learn. When it is harder only because the numbers grew, you can only feel that you are losing.

## How Inkisle does it

[Inkisle](https://inkisle.ipe.rest) is built as a sequence of seas. Clear a round and the next one opens, and every sea adds one rule. The rules for the basic game fit on one page: islands make troops, and a larger force arriving takes the island. Further rules arrive one at a time, in their own sea, so the plan is that nobody is handed a manual.

That is the design, and we should be clear about what it is. Inkisle is in development, and the exact rules each sea will add are still being worked out and tested. We are describing the approach, not a finished list.

## How the others deal with it

Our other games arrive at the question from different directions.

[Iltonggi](https://iltonggi.ipe.rest) lets you choose a kingdom and a difficulty at the start, and the first game is a short 20 turns with no enemy attack in the first two rounds. Its blog also tells how the game grew: the first build on TestFlight could do little more than choose a kingdom and a difficulty and play one round, and features were added one by one after that. The game was built up in the same spirit, one thing at a time.

[Claimball](https://claimball.ipe.rest) plans to grow through its rivals. The plan is to group them into leagues, so that the step up is a stronger opponent, not a new manual. The number of rivals and leagues is a proposal before playtesting, so we do not quote it.

## The limit of this idea

Adding rules one at a time has an obvious risk. If every sea adds a rule, the later ones can end up heavy. We do not yet know whether Inkisle avoids that, and nobody can until they have played it. A curve that looks smooth in a design document can still have a bump. We would rather admit this now than discover it in public later.

If you like the idea of a strategy game that teaches in small steps, Inkisle is the card to open on [play.ipe.rest](https://play.ipe.rest).

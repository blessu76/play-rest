Tottertop asks for one thing from your thumb: a tap. A crane swings a block from side to side, you tap, the block falls, and the tower either holds or leans a little further. We are aiming for rounds of about a minute.

## Why one tap

The smaller the control, the sooner a game can start. There is nothing to learn about buttons, so you can begin in a queue, at a bus stop or between two messages. One tap also makes the game about a single decision, when, and that decision is the whole skill.

## Why a wobble

The tower is a physics toy, and the fun is in how it fails. A block placed slightly off the middle shifts the weight, and the tower leans and sways before it settles or goes down. We would like the swing to grow tenser as the tower gets taller, so a calm tower becomes a tense one without any rule being added.

## Why a minute

Short rounds forgive. When a tower falls you should have lost about a minute, not an evening, and starting again should cost one tap. We are also thinking about the moment of the fall, and would like you to be able to see it happen instead of the game cutting away.

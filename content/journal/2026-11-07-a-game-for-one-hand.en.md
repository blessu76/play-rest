Look at where spare minutes actually happen. You stand in a line with a bag on one shoulder. You hold a rail with one hand. You carry a cup, or an umbrella, or a child's mitten. In a surprising number of the moments when we reach for a game, one hand is already busy.

That is a design constraint, and it is easy to ignore, because games are usually made and tested at a desk with both hands free.

## What one hand changes

Playing with a single thumb takes away a lot of what many games rely on. There is no second hand for a second button. There is no landscape grip. A small screen held upright is the whole stage, and a thumb can only reach so far.

What is left is a small set of gestures that mean something. A thumb can drag. A thumb can tap. If a game cannot be played with those two things, it asks too much of the moments it lives in.

## How Inkisle does it

[Inkisle](https://inkisle.ipe.rest) is a light island-conquest strategy game for your phone: you send fleets between islands and tint the sea your color. A round takes about 3 to 5 minutes and is played upright with one hand. To send a fleet you drag from one of your islands to another, and a bar at the bottom lets you choose how much to send.

Dragging and choosing an amount are the controls the game's site describes. Islands make troops, and a larger force arriving takes the island. The rules are short enough that one-hand input does not have to hide anything.

Inkisle also needs no account and no network. When a spare moment turns up, nothing stands between you and the first drag.

## An honest caveat

Inkisle runs in real time, and a game that moves while you watch is harder to play one-handed than one that waits. We have designed for it, but we have not verified how it feels on a moving train or with a bag swinging off your elbow, and we are not going to pretend that we have. A thumb-sized target is a promise on paper until real hands have used it.

It is also still in development. There is no store page or release date, and the price is not set before launch.

## Smaller than the moment

The size of a game's controls should be smaller than the moment it lives in. If a spare minute is mostly a hand and a thumb, then the whole game should fit there. When we think about whether Inkisle succeeds, that is the question we ask: can it be played in the space that remains?
